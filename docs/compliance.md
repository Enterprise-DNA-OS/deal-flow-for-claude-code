# Record checks and their sources

`/compliance` (`npm run deals -- compliance`) checks the records against the rules below. It finds records that are missing, out of date or inconsistent. It does not decide whether an offer is lawful, whether an investor qualifies, or whether anyone holds inside information. Those calls belong to the people running the fund and their lawyers. Sources checked 10 October 2026.

| Rule | What the check finds | Source and limit |
|---|---|---|
| NZ-FMCA-CERT | A soft or committed investor relying on an NZ safe harbour or eligible investor certificate signed more than two years ago | The FMA's [summary of Schedule 1 of the Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013](https://www.fma.govt.nz/assets/Information-sheets/141101-Exclusions-under-the-Financial-Markets-Conduct-Act-2013-FMC-Act-summary-of-schedule-1.pdf) says the safe harbour certificate is valid for two years, and an eligible investor certification is valid for two years. A certificate can be revoked earlier in writing; record that with `commit --certificate=none`. |
| AU-S708-CERT | A soft or committed investor relying on an Australian qualified accountant's certificate signed more than two years ago | Corporations Act 2001 (Cth) s708(8)(c): the certificate must be given no more than two years before the offer, as summarised in the government's [impact analysis for the 2026 qualified accountant instrument](https://oia.pmc.gov.au/sites/default/files/posts/2026/09/IA%20Dashboard%20%26%20Statement%20of%20sufficiency_0.pdf). ASIC's page on [certificates issued by a qualified accountant](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/financial-services/financial-product-disclosure/certificates-issued-by-a-qualified-accountant/). The check measures two years from the signing date you record; the test is against the offer date. |
| CERT-MISSING | A committed investor with no certificate on file | Internal control. Not every investor needs one (other exclusions exist); record `other` with a date when a different basis applies. |
| INSIDER-LIST | An open deal on a listed company that is not flagged as restricted | Internal control supporting the insider conduct rules in Part 5 of the FMC Act 2013 ([FMA information sheet on insider trading](https://www.fma.govt.nz/library/reports-and-papers/shadow-insider-trading/)) and Part 7.10 of the Corporations Act 2001 (Cth). The flag is a record; it does not stop anyone trading. |
| NDA-DILIGENCE | A deal in diligence, at committee or at term sheet with no signed, unexpired NDA with the company | Internal policy. Change the stages it applies to with `/customise`. |
| IC-RECORD | A deal at term sheet or closed with no recorded invest decision | Internal governance. Imported closed deals usually need their committee record added by hand. |
| PRIVACY-REVIEW | A person nobody has touched in three years whose record has no retention review in that time | NZ Privacy Act 2020 [information privacy principle 9](https://privacy.org.nz/privacy-act-2020/privacy-principles/9): do not keep personal information longer than it is needed. Australian Privacy Principle 11.2: destroy or de-identify personal information no longer needed ([OAIC APP guidelines, chapter 11](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-11-app-11-security-of-personal-information)). Neither law sets three years; that is a default review period, set yours with `/customise`. Record a review in `people.privacy_reviewed_on`. |

There is no delete command. Removing a person's details after a review is a deliberate database change made by the operator, recorded in the activity log.

The attention list (`/attention`) adds operating checks with no legal source: deals quiet for 21 days, overdue next steps and follow-ups, NDAs lapsing within 30 days, late portfolio reports and certificates expiring within 60 days.
